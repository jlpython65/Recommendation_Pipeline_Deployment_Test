This video discusses Jean Valjean's journey in Victor Hugo's Les Misérables as an illustration of receiving and extending grace. After spending nineteen years in prison for stealing bread to feed his starving family, Valjean leaves bitter and distrustful of a society that has treated him harshly.

His encounter with a bishop becomes a turning point. After Valjean steals the bishop's silver and is caught, the bishop tells the authorities that the silver was a gift and gives Valjean the candlesticks as well. This unexpected forgiveness challenges Valjean's resentment and inspires him to change his life.

Valjean begins again under a new identity and tries to live honestly and serve his community. Having received compassion, he extends it to others: he takes responsibility for Fantine's daughter, Cosette, and makes sacrifices to care for her.

The session connects Valjean's story to the Christian understanding of grace: undeserved compassion can transform a person and inspire them to show compassion to others, including people who are outcast or hurting. It invites viewers to reflect on how they have received grace and whether they are willing to extend it.
