In Les Miserables, Jean Valjean knows the right thing to do. Ideally, doing the right thing would be easy. But in the book Valjean shows a lot of resistance in doing so. Well what does "the right thing" mean here? Why is Valjean's path to being a more loving and courageous man deemed as redemptive? 

The idea of redemption differs across different belief systems. In this case, Les Miserables' definition of redemption is inherited from Christianity ([[Other Types of Redemption]]) where sin is embodied by self-centered attitudes. So virtue in this case should be attitudes that embody love and compassion. 

Valjean's conscience's morality revolves around the Christian ideals of love. This is why he asks Cossette to forgive the Thernadiers (goodness no!), disobeys laws to help others, feel conflicted on whether to save Champmathieu or not. 

But wouldn't living without a conscience make his life better? Guilt is the consequence for holding onto Christian values or caring for others and we see how that emotion drives his actions. If he stripped away the guilt from sacraficing Champmathieu, he can live happily as mayor. We don't know how Valjean reached his final decision near the end, but we can infer that if he didn't turn himself in, he would have felt like he is living a lie. Later in the book, if he kept Cossette at the convent, she would stay with him. If he let Marius die, Cossette would stay with him. What about the countless other times he risks so much to help others? Valjean could've lived a simple life focused on self-preservation and Cossette. 

So what does he have to gain with a conscience? Peace of mind in being virtuous and embracing his capacity to love. Valjean loves Cossette and later loves Marius and his family. He is also loving towards the poor. He finds meaning in his ability to love other people. To some, this is an existence that feels right.

As much as Valjean is a paragon of virtue, his trauma plays a key part in his self-sacrificial nature and corrupted his conscience. He has internalized the belief that he is an unlovable convict who deserves punishment after 20 years in prison. His family is gone, society has shunned him away, and even when he . When he can finally stay with Cossette after her marriage, he decided to cut himself from her life. He believes he doesn't deserve to be happy because conscience demands that the right thing is to sacrifice himself. If he truly loves Cossette and Marius, he would never deceive them and let them be around someone so worthless. 

This is where i have to disagree with his "conscience". Valjean hasn't healed from 20 years of trauma. As much as he loved others, he failed to love himself. Seeing that makes me question what's driving my conscience. 

Overall, Valjean's character has taught me about how to live with a conscience. Living without one could lead to an empty existence devoid of love. Though living with one is painful, there is meaning in following virtue if we define virtue as loving others. However, we need to be careful that our conscience is not driven by overwhelming guilt or self-loathing. In that case, we'll be enslaved to our conscience, depriving ourselves of happiness we may or may not deserve.
