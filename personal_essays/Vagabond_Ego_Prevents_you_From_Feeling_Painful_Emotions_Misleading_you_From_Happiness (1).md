Men are taught to strive towards strength. That ideal defines our worth in society. But the meaning of that word can be warped due to how we deal with our trauma. Takezo believed strength allows you not to feel pain. Yet he is deemed the weakest according to Takuan. But Otsu, even if her emotions compell her to lose temper, was deemed the strongest. Though strength can take on different meanings, a universal meaning would be a quality that allows one to change something to remove an unfavorable outcome. Whether that would be within or outside yourself. But how come Otsu's version of strength is far more accomplished than Takezo's? Takezo's idea of strength is a defense mechanism that prevents him from feeling painful emotions like griefm fear, hurt, and guilt. Being unable to accept these vulnerable emotions works against the whole goal of his "invincibility" ideal. To be able to bear pain.

But how would Takezo even come to this realization? All the people he have killed and the villager's reaction to his kills provide personal and social proof that he is "invincible". All he did was bolster his ego and the ego loves to continue reinforcing itself. It wasn't until that he was captured and strung up to a tree that he has nothing to do but questioned how he got there in the first place. This means confronting traumatizing memories and the emotions attached to them. He remembered being abandoned by his mother and shunned by his father. When he returns to the present, he feels despair. He feels grief from failing to live up to his invincible ideal. From that grief is fear of not dying a warrior's death. The only thing he wanted, a death that validated his existence and ideal. There is a smidge of guilt for killing all those people as well. 

When Takezo finally gets down from the tree, he's getting what we wanted. A death by someone's hand. It's not from a warrior, but from a monk. Not in a dual, but Takezo at this point wil ltake anything he can get. But Takezo wasn't rejoicing. Though proving himself felt good for his ego, Takezo still felt empty.  All of his striving felt meaningless. You can say his ego has led him astray. Once you peel back his ego, you're left with a man in deep pain. His existence is now fileld with nothing but pain. So he despairs and tries to bash his head against a rock. 

The ego, once threatened, will employ secondary emotions like anger to help you avoid those emotions. The ego builds an ideal to strive towards to ensure continue avoidance. But once you're prevented from following said ideal and eventually fail, all these emotions come back to haunt you. In Takezo's case, he believes being invincible would help him deal with the pain of living. But it only misled him. By the time he realizes, it feels too late and he starts questioning his existence.
